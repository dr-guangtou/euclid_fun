Contaminants of faint light

  • VIS stray light (10–15% of zodiacal light, depends on attitude and detector region, +14%/yr); zodiacal gradients; Galactic cirrus (EDF-N limits 0.2–0.4 mag brighter than EDF-S/F).
  • VIS dichroic ghost ~70″ from stars with G <~ 14 (only flagged); NIR ghosts and ~1.7′ arcs around stars brighter than ~11 mag; unmodelled internal-reflection arcs.
  • NIR persistence (masked, ~5% of pixels), persistence-like background structure, 1/f row/column stripes, detector offsets.
  • Small PSF models (VIS 2.1″, NIR ≤ 4.5″ stamps): no extended wings. Build your own from stars, or use the ERO extended PSF.
  • Bright-star masks cover cores and spikes only; the VMPZ effective coverage deliberately masks bright SGA galaxies.

Lessons from the Euclid cluster/ICL team

  • Subtract one sky on a scale of several times the object (1 Mpc boxes for clusters). Mask in two zones (+50% outside, +25% inside the diffuse region), and protect the target with an adaptive-smoothing mask.
  • Use elliptical annuli with sigma-clipped medians (AutoProf, regularised shapes). Estimate errors from random blank-sky apertures plus mock injection into real Euclid sky.
  • HE (or summed YJH) reaches furthest at z > 0.2; IE wins at z < 0.2; YE is the noisiest.
  • Masks bias measured shapes (mask-defined diffuse light is more elliptical), so forward-model your selection.

Reproducing the ICL team's processing (Bamford et al.) with public DR1

Every input is public and downloads anonymously (checked 2026-11-13):

  • VIS calibrated frames with BKG/WGT (42,092);
  • NIR calibrated frames with BKG (71,659);
  • NISP spectroscopic frames for the persistence model (26,581);
  • LE1 raw frames (201,082);
  • exposure times (start_time_mjd, MJD-OBS).

One NIR H frame is 0.8 GB (49 HDUs, electrons, sky ~65 e− still in SCI), and its BKG file is another 0.8 GB. The Q1 test cluster now has 13 DR1 observations. The team's code (github.com/IntraClusterLight/nicl_q1, Apache-2.0) is a Q1 snapshot. Its "DR1" mode points to the Consortium-internal archive (easidr, sedm schema), so switch it to eas.esac.esa.int, the dr1. tables and release=dr1. For a cluster-sized field, whole files of the neighbouring exposures come to ~300 GB of VIS and ~40 GB per NIR band, which makes ESA Datalabs the practical place to run it. For a single galaxy, see the next table. The Euclid cluster catalogue they used comes only with full DR1, so use eRASS1, redMaPPer, SPT or ACT clusters.

The ~300 GB figure applies to cluster-sized fields that fill the focal plane, as in Bamford et al. For a ~1′ galaxy:

  • VIS: the MER mosaic with BGMOD restored is adequate up to ~1′ (Bamford et al.). Use the cutout service for BGSUB and a row-range read for BGMOD.
  • NIR: take level A or B from the frames, whose SCI still contains the sky. Co-add the dithers and fit one large-scale sky.
  • Atemporal model: it describes the detectors, not the target. Compute it once per period and sky region (e.g. in Datalabs) and reuse it for all your galaxies.
