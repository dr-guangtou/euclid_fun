# Post-processing of Euclid Q1 calibrated frames for ICL and LSB measurements (Bamford et al. 2026)

## TL;DR
The Q1 cluster team found that standard Euclid mosaics (MER) remove diffuse light on scales above about 10 arcsec in VIS and above about 0.5 arcmin in NIR, because background is subtracted twice at small scales (once per dithered exposure by the VIS/NIR pipelines, once on tiles by MER). They rebuilt images from the level-2 (LE2) calibrated single-exposure frames with additive corrections (atemporal background, NIR row/column "tartan" pattern, NIR persistence, detector-to-detector background matching) and only then subtract a large-scale background (1 Mpc boxes, e.g. 9 arcmin at z=0.1). Injection tests with mock clusters show faint outskirts are preserved down to the noise floor: 3 sigma limits on arcmin scales of about 27.5 mag/arcsec2 in YE, JE, HE and about 29 in IE. For anyone doing LSB photometry of large galaxies on Euclid data this is the key reference on what is wrong with the default products and what can be done.

## Key facts and numbers
- Data: Euclid Q1, 63 deg2 in the three Euclid Deep Fields (EDF-N, EDF-S, EDF-F) at nominal EWS depth (p. 3). Each EWS observation = reference observing sequence (ROS) of four dithered nominal exposures per band (IE, YE, JE, HE) plus two short IE exposures that they do not use (p. 3). Dithers about 2 arcmin; observations at least 20 arcmin apart (p. 17).
- Pixel scales: VIS 0.1, NISP 0.3 arcsec/pixel; PSF 0.15 arcsec (VIS) and 0.45 arcsec (NIR) (p. 1). Their resampling target is 0.3 arcsec/pixel for all bands, zero point 23.9 (1 ADU = 1 microJy) (p. 22).
- Test fields: observation 2683, cluster MCXC J1754.6+6803 at z=0.077 (p. 2, Fig. 1); mock tests on 11 observations over 40 x 40 arcmin in EDF-S (p. 4); noise tests on 2.5 x 2.5 deg images in EDF-S (and each of three fields) (p. 5, 8).
- Surface-brightness limits (3 sigma, from scatter of median flux in many randomly placed masked square apertures; MAD scaled to sigma; sigma-clipped; measured after a 9 arcmin background box with 3x3 median filter) (p. 5):
  - Calibrated frames combined with no extra processing: detections limited to brighter than about 26.3 mag/arcsec2 (VIS) and about 25.5 (NIR), at any aperture size (p. 6).
  - Their post-processing improves this by about 1.5 mag (VIS) and about 1 mag (NIR) without any small-scale background subtraction (p. 6).
  - 3 sigma, 10 x 10 arcsec box: 27.8 mag/arcsec2 in IE and 26.4 in YE/JE/HE (p. 7). The naive white-noise extrapolation (noise scaling as 1/sqrt(N pixels)) would give about 30.0 (VIS) and 28.5 (NIR), which is more than 2 mag too optimistic (p. 6-7). Related work: realistic dwarf-galaxy profiles with r_h about 10 arcsec reach 26-27 mag/arcsec2 in MER VIS images (p. 7).
  - ERO LSB processing (Cuillandre et al. 2025) quoted as 28.3 (IE) and 26.8 (HE), 3 sigma in 10 x 10 arcsec after a 0.4 mag correction and a 26 arcsec background; theoretical photon-noise-only limits converted to standard magnitudes: 29.5 (IE), 28.0 (HE) (p. 7, footnote 4).
  - Several arcminutes: reliable detection of about 27.5 mag/arcsec2 in YE/JE/HE and about 29 in IE (abstract; p. 11). Elliptical annuli (ellipticity 0.5, width r_c/3) on 2.5 deg images: 3 sigma limits plotted in Fig. 5 (p. 8), analytic fit by symbolic regression (PySR) with RMS residual 0.003 mag/arcsec2.
- Galactic cirrus sets the large-scale floor even at high latitude: arcmin-scale limit in EDF-N (b = 30 deg, E(B-V) about 0.06) is brighter than EDF-S/EDF-F by about 0.4 (IE) and 0.2 (HE) mag/arcsec2; EDF-S brighter than EDF-F (E(B-V) about 0.02 vs 0.01; b = -47 vs -55 deg) by about 0.1 (IE), 0.05 (HE) (p. 8).
- Measurement accuracy on 776 Hydrangea mock clusters (z about 0.1-0.8, M200 = 1e14-1e15 Msun), HE band (p. 9-11): surface brightness about 10 percent, PA about 10 deg (3 deg for BCGs at z about 0.2, 15 deg for ICL at z about 0.8), ellipticity about 0.1 (scatter 0.04 inner to 0.12 at 0.1 R200).
- Cost: full post-processing of Q1 takes a few days on 16 cores (p. 3).

## Data products and data model
Used by the team (all publicly available from the Euclid Science Archive for Q1, p. 3 footnote 2):
- LE2 calibrated frames (individual exposures), multi-extension FITS; per detector unit one science image, one noise map, one pixel-flag extension. Detector unit = quadrant of a CCD for VIS, a whole detector for NIR (2040 x 2040 pixels) (p. 3-4). NIR spectroscopic (SIR) exposures were also used, to model persistence (p. 20).
- Inverse-variance maps (from noise maps) and quality flags to build weights; invalid pixels get weight zero (p. 22).
- Standard Q1 MER tiles, plus the MER-estimated background model (restorable for VIS; for NIR the per-exposure background cannot be restored, p. 2).
- Q1 MER catalogue source information is not the focus.

Produced (not part of DR1; code released as nicl, github.com/IntraClusterLight/nicl_q1; the team does not intend to tile the survey; p. 3): per-cluster 30 x 30 arcmin combined images per band at 0.3 arcsec/pixel, YJHE combined image, ICL / measurement / background masks, elliptical-isophote profiles. Their Q1 products are not expected in DR1; DR1 ICL work will use the same recipe (p. 3).

## Processing and methods
Starting point is LE2 frames, which are flat-fielded and flux calibrated, then:
1. Atemporal background (A.1, p. 17-18). Remaining detector-scale structure after LE2 (about 10 percent of sky in VIS, about 20 percent in NIR; quadrant-to-quadrant offsets) is estimated as the source-masked median over a group of neighbouring observations (N_obs = 6 NIR, 10 VIS; target excluded; truncated at field ends). Done in two stages: coarsen each exposure by median in boxes (NIR 51 x 51, VIS 149 x 149 pixels; the NIR grid subsamples the 204 x 204 pixel illumination-correction grid by 4), then median across exposures. Subtract each coarsened frame's median before combining and add back one scalar level afterwards (otherwise a gradient is introduced when SWarp resamples with distortion). Uncoarsened by nearest pixel (NIR) or linear (VIS) interpolation.
2. Tartan (A.2, p. 18-19; NIR only): row and column background banding, varying exposure to exposure, probably readout correlation (1/f, cross-talk). Model: mask sources, subtract a 200-pixel circular median filter to remove large-scale structure (bands are about 100 pixels or narrower), take masked median along each row and each column, coadd, remove mean, subtract.
3. Persistence (A.3, p. 20-21; NIR only): streaks from bright sources in the interleaved grism (SIR) exposures, plus compact circular features from earlier JE/HE/YE exposures. Q1 SGS only masks where predicted persistence is above 2 electrons from the previous spectroscopic exposure (removes few features). They estimate the signal from nine temporally neighbouring same-band exposures (four either side plus target): second-lowest pixel value over the stack, after masking pixels by when a persistence-causing source above 10 000 e-/pixel was recorded. Rescale to the target time with a power-law decay f1 = f0 (t0/t1)^a, a = 1.0 (scatter 0.2). Subtract, mask pixels whose correction uncertainty exceeds 20 e-, then re-estimate atemporal background and tartan. Persistence in VIS negligible.
4. Resample (A.4, p. 21-22): SWarp, LANCZOS2, onto a common grid with detectors from dithers overlapping the target area expanded by one dither offset (needed for step 5); no cropping at this stage. The LANCZOS2 kernel spreads a bad NIR pixel to about 16 pixels (conservative, leaves masked holes). Geometric distortion changes pixel solid angle by about 1 percent (NIR) and about 2 percent (VIS), which is why background must be near zero before resampling.
5. Background matching (A.5, p. 22): additive constant per detector image found by least squares from differences of all overlapping detector pairs (C_j - C_i = Delta_ij), weights from inverse variances, source-masked difference images; rank-deficient by one degree of freedom, resolved by zero mean correction. Works even when a source is larger than one detector because it uses overlaps between different dithers. Fixing small artefacts (persistence) improves flatness above 10 arcmin.
6. Combine (A.6, p. 22-23): SWarp CLIPPED weighted mean (not the MEDIAN mode used for Q1 MER stacks, which is more robust to artefacts but noisier). No local background subtraction yet.
7. Final background (Sect. 4.1, App. B.1, p. 7, 23): photutils Background2D (SExtractor-style sigma-clipped median in boxes, 3x3 median filter, bicubic interpolation), with sources masked. Box = 1 Mpc at cluster redshift (9 arcmin = 1800 pixels at z=0.1) on 30 x 30 arcmin cutouts so at least a 3 x 3 grid fits. Larger boxes only slightly increase scatter at large radius.
8. Masking (Sect. 4.2, B.2, p. 7-8, 23-24): three masks (bad pixels; regular objects detected at 3 sigma over at least 5 pixels with 500-pixel background boxes, deblended, grown iteratively to 150 percent size; central objects inside the ICL region found at 2 sigma over a local background at 0.25 sigma_s, deblended aggressively, grown to 125 percent). An "ICL mask" from adaptive smoothing (binary search on the Gaussian-median smoothing scale until the central source is just detected at 2 sigma) protects the BCG+ICL from being masked as sources; background mask = measurement mask + ICL mask. NIR uses one mask from a combined YJHE image (weights 0.375, 0.358, 0.267 for YE, JE, HE). Detailed choices are not critical because the statistics are robust.
9. Photometry (Sect. 4.3, B.3, p. 8, 24): AutoProf (Stone et al. 2021) elliptical isophotes, centre and semi-major axes fixed (start 1 pixel, each 40 percent larger), ellipticity and PA free, background fixed (not re-estimated), S/N threshold for shape lowered from 2 to 1. Flux per annulus from masked iterative 3 sigma-clipped median (photutils). Colour profiles use ellipses defined on the YJHE image applied to all bands including IE. Elliptical (not circular) annuli are preferred, since with circular annuli masks in different azimuths bias the mean and sigma-clipping fails.
10. Uncertainty: background-level uncertainty from random elliptical apertures (Sect. 4.4) combined in quadrature with standard error of pixels in each annulus (p. 8-9).

## Validation and performance
- Injection of a smooth BCG+ICL mock (z about 0.1) into four positions in LE2 frames, full pipeline: profile recovered to about 28 mag/arcsec2 (radius about 7 arcmin in HE) i.e. down to the 3 sigma background limit (Fig. 3, p. 5).
- Noise vs aperture size compared against: no processing, MER with background restored, ideal 1/sqrt(N) (Fig. 4, p. 6). For apertures up to about 10 pixels, noise within 0.1 mag of MER; for VIS their large-aperture statistics match MER only after also subtracting a 26 arcsec background; NIR slightly better than MER (artefact removal) (p. 7). MER NIR single-pixel noise is lower by about 0.44 mag/arcsec2 (factor 2/3) because of bilinear resampling (pixel correlation); not seen in VIS since their 0.3 arcsec pixels undersample (p. 6).
- Mock Hydrangea clusters injected in real processed images (Sect. 5, p. 9-11): flux recovered within 2 percent for annulus S/N above about 100; median bias +3 percent at S/N = 3 (galaxy outskirts leaking in); scatter about -10 percent / +20 percent at S/N = 3; with satellites included bias up to +14 percent at S/N = 3, and ellipticity +0.08 higher at 0.1 R200 (because stripped light around satellites is counted as ICL). These are definitional as well as masking effects and matter when comparing with simulations.
- Masking trade-off: larger masks reduce contamination but remove area and may bias against satellites along the major axis (p. 11).

## Known issues, caveats, limitations
Issues found in the standard Q1 processing and data (with causes as stated by the authors):
- Double background subtraction removes diffuse flux (p. 2, Fig. 1, p. 7). The VIS/NIR pipelines estimate a small-scale background on every dithered exposure and subtract it in mosaicking; MER then subtracts a further background in 26 arcsec boxes with 3x3 median filter (Romelli et al. 2026, sect. 3.3). Photometry of sources below about 10 arcsec is accurate; larger objects lose flux. The second (MER) background is delivered and can be restored. This is enough for VIS LSB sources up to about 1 arcmin, but in NIR the first, per-exposure background cannot be restored, so Q1 MER does not allow VIS+NIR LSB studies above about 0.5 arcmin (p. 2). DR1 SGS improves NIR background subtraction, but dedicated processing is still needed above about 1 arcmin (p. 3).
- The apparently low noise of MER on large apertures is an artefact of this subtraction; the data are not usable there (p. 6-7).
- Residual background structure in calibrated frames (p. 17): illumination corrections from LED flats plus a 76-point dither star-based correction on a roughly 1 arcmin grid; NIR correction reveals about 5 percent additive structure on sub-detector scales, probably diffuse charge persistence, yet applied multiplicatively; VIS shows about 2 percent variations between and within quadrants, also applied multiplicatively (best for compact sources, not for background flatness).
- NIR tartan (row/column) banding varying between exposures; NIR persistence streaks and circular features, diagonal in mosaics, visible in LE2 and MER (Fig. 1); the Q1 SGS persistence mask removes only a small fraction; persistence model parameters vary with time and cannot be calibrated retroactively; sources saturated several hours earlier cannot be modelled (p. 20).
- Detector-to-detector offsets and gradients within chips (Figs. A.1, A.2, p. 18).
- Remaining sources of background variation they did not remove: extended PSF wings of very bright stars, scattered light from outside the field of view, zodiacal light, Galactic cirrus (dominant at large scales even at high latitude), clustering of faint unresolved galaxies (p. 7). No ghost or PSF-wing subtraction was performed on the data; the PSF with large-scale wings was only used to convolve the mock clusters (Appendix C, p. 24; PSF from Cuillandre et al. 2025).
- The bad-pixel footprint is enlarged by about 16 times in NIR with LANCZOS2 resampling (p. 22).
- Their own limits: no deblending or modelling of satellite haloes, only masking; the ICL mask may be incomplete for very large nearby clusters or objects larger than the Euclid FoV (extra-wide sampling of N_obs observations needed; footnote 6, p. 17); a background box scale of about 1 Mpc is the safe choice, and smaller boxes eat the ICL.
- Q1 specifics: in Q1 each field has a distinct observation-ID sequence used for the atemporal median; DR1 needs a time and angular-separation criterion (p. 17).

## Implications for extended-galaxy photometry, 2-D modelling and low-surface-brightness work
- Default MER tiles are unsafe for any target larger than about 10 arcsec if you care about its outskirts: outer Sersic components, stellar haloes, tidal features and ICL are all partly subtracted. The same applies to PSF-wing and sky determination in 2-D fits: a bad sky in the tile biases Sersic index and effective radius high or low and truncates outer discs. Check whether a MER background model is provided and restore it, but note it is incomplete for NIR in Q1.
- The central result: avoid small-scale background subtraction altogether, flatten with additive detector-level corrections, then subtract one large-scale background tailored to the target (several times the object size, with sources and the object itself masked). Use the injection of a realistic mock profile into the frames to test this for your galaxy.
- Realistic limits to quote: 3 sigma in 10 x 10 arcsec of 27.8 mag/arcsec2 (IE) and 26.4 (NIR) for a typical position in the EDF-S; arcmin scales about 29 (IE) and 27.5 (YJH). Cirrus (EDF-N brighter than S and F) is the floor, so expect field-to-field variation of 0.1-0.4 mag/arcsec2. Beware extrapolating pixel noise to large apertures.
- For nearby large galaxies (several arcmin) the box-based background approach demands more care than for clusters at z about 0.1-0.8: the box size must exceed the galaxy plus halo by a good margin and the cutout must hold at least a 3 x 3 grid, i.e. about 3 times the box, probably beyond the field of one pointing for the largest galaxies; that is a limitation not tested here (footnote 6).
- Masking recipe transfers directly: detect at 3 sigma over at least 5 pixels, deblend, grow by 50 percent for sources outside the diffuse region, grow by 25 percent inside it, protect the target with an adaptive-smoothing mask; measure with elliptical, sigma-clipped medians. Use YJHE-defined ellipses for colour profiles.
- Elliptical annuli with fixed centre and free ellipticity/PA via AutoProf are a validated non-parametric profile method here; relevant to the group's Sersic and LSB profile work as a cross-check.
- Not addressed: ghosts, bright-star halo subtraction, flat-field residuals beyond additive atemporal terms, bulge/disc modelling, tidal-feature detection algorithms. The paper does not provide a PSF-wing subtraction method.

## Practical recommendations for users
What a DR1 user can reproduce with public products:
- Steps 1-6 and the final background subtraction and masking need only LE2 calibrated frames (science, noise, flag extensions for VIS and NIR, and for persistence the SIR frames), i.e. the individual exposures that DR1 provides, plus SWarp, photutils and AutoProf; the nicl code (github.com/IntraClusterLight/nicl_q1) implements them for Q1 and the authors say they will base DR1 ICL work on the same recipe. Expect to retrieve many neighbouring observations (6 NIR / 10 VIS plus overlaps) for the atemporal median. Whether DR1 LE2 frames keep the same layout and the updated NIR background step should be verified in the DR1 documentation.
- Check which of these Q1 problems were fixed in DR1 SGS (the authors note improved NIR background subtraction) before applying their tartan/atemporal corrections blindly; the dedicated processing is still needed above 1 arcmin.
- For VIS only and targets below about 1 arcmin, the standard MER tile with the MER background model restored may suffice (their statement p. 2); for NIR no.
Do and don't:
- Do not trust 1/sqrt(N) extrapolations; measure the noise in random masked apertures at your own scale.
- Do not use circular annuli on elliptical LSB structures with masks.
- Do not use the MEDIAN-combine MER stack if you need the best S/N for LSB; a clipped weighted mean from frames is preferable but demands cleaner inputs.
- Do keep the mask restrained near the target (the ICL-protect mask) and report the definition of diffuse light used when comparing with simulations.
- Do inject mock profiles into the frames to validate your pipeline.
- Their products (cluster cutouts, masks) were produced only for Q1 clusters and are not expected in DR1.
