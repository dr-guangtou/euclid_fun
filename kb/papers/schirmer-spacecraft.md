# Euclid spacecraft performance at L2: stray light, contamination, thermal stability, guiding, impacts (Schirmer et al. 2026)

## TL;DR
After about three years in orbit (data to Sept/Oct 2026), the spacecraft is in excellent shape (99.13% of planned data retrieved, p. 1-2). For image-quality and LSB users the key points are: (i) residual solar stray light inside the VIS cavity is only about 10-15% of the zodiacal level after the azimuth-angle fix, but grows by about 14% per year (meteoroid punctures of the insulation) and depends on AA/SAA (p. 4-5); (ii) heavy water-ice contamination on folding mirror FoM3 between 14 Mar and 8 Jun 2024 cost up to 0.6+ mag of VIS throughput with a strong focal-plane gradient, and about 300 deg^2 of VIS data were excluded from DR1 (p. 12-13, 28); (iii) NISP detectors keep accumulating ice (about 10 nm/month), giving a slowly varying quantum efficiency (p. 15-16); (iv) the telescope defocus follows the solar aspect angle with a 21-46 h thermal response (p. 7-9); (v) out-of-field stray light was overestimated pre-launch by factors 4 (HE) to 27 (IE) (p. 18); (vi) guiding is excellent (RPE sigma about 8 and 5 mas for tip/tilt axes, p. 21). It is the main source on the physical origin of background and ghost behaviour.

## Key facts and numbers
- Operations: 99.13% of pre-planned science and telemetry files retrieved between Feb 2024 and Jun 2026; operational time > 99% (p. 1-2). Data loss from solar X-rays about 2% (p. 6).
- Orbit: eclipse-free halo around Sun-Earth L2, period about 180 days; co-rotating velocity 0.16-0.34 km/s; unstable with e-folding about 23 days; orbit-correction manoeuvres (OCMs) via Floquet method; long-term cumulative delta-v 0.539-0.554 m/s per year (p. 2-4). SOPS windows every 4 weeks, every 8 weeks from 2026-01-26 (p. 3).
- Consumables: hydrazine 137 kg initially, lasts to about 2105 (usable until ~2108 in Fig. 2); cold-gas (70 kg N2) depleted about May 2040 (p. 3-4, 29).
- Solar stray light into VIS: enters via sunlight scattered off an illuminated thruster nozzle onto the reflective back of the sunshield, then through small openings/holes in the MLI into the instrument cavity (p. 3-4). Off-setting the azimuth angle (AA) to put the nozzle in shadow reduced the background by factors 100-1000 to about 10-15% of the zodiacal background level, at a cost of about 5% survey area (extra overlaps) (p. 4).
- VIS master-dark background (closed shutter; 60-70 darks over 7-14 days; dark current negligible, at least 40x below measured signal, so darks = pure stray light) in four quadrant groups A-D (Table 2, p. 6): B0 (Aug 2023) = 2.92, 7.46, 8.94, 12.00 x 10^-3 e-/s; slopes (4.18, 10.44, 12.54, 15.86) x 10^-4 e-/s/yr = 14.3, 14.0, 14.0, 13.2 %/yr; total increase 38.1% between Aug 2023 and May 2026 (13.9% per yr), after correction by (d_h/d_h0)^2 (p. 4-5). Groups C and D (higher absolute stray light) respond more to higher SAA (Fig. 5 caption).
- Effective hole area to explain stray light at AA = 0 in Aug 2023: A_h about 3-30 mm^2 (6.4 mm^2 nominal estimate, Appendix B, p. 36); observed growth corresponds to 0.41-4.1 mm^2/yr, consistent with meteoroids of diameter >~ 40 um (p. 6).
- MLI stack around the cavity: 25 um perforated Kapton+VDA, 19 x 7.6 um perforated polyimide layers, 40 um black Kapton (not perforated); total polyimide 209 um, 303 g/m^2 (p. 6).
- Solar X-rays: penetrate sunshield and insulation and reach the VIS detectors with attenuation 10^2 to at least 10^8 at 6 keV; patterns are attitude-dependent "tomographic images" of the sunshield; strongest flares (e.g. 2025-05-15 X-class) saturate VIS in low-attenuation areas; proton flux can rise 2-3 days after a major flare (p. 6). Sunspot number dropped by almost 50% from mid-2024 to June 2026; lost VIS quadrants declined correspondingly (Fig. 6). Radiation storms Nov 2025 (S3, G4, strongest GLE since 2005) and Jan 2026 (S4, soft spectrum) (p. 6).
- Thermal: baseplate (SiC, 2.5 x 2.1 m, 187 kg) temperature about 134.86-135.04 K; defocus z about 0.32-0.56 um across attitudes (Fig. 7); T and z follow SAA much more than AA unless SAA >~ 100 deg; annual modulation from heliocentric distance 31 mK and 33 nm; z follows T at 1.28 nm/mK on short timescales but long-term drifts are -1.48 nm/mK (T +16.2 mK/yr, z -23.9 nm/yr) (p. 7-8, Table 3). Fit used data after 2024-04-01 (T) and after 2024-07-01 (defocus) because of ice on FoM3 (p. 7-8).
- Thermal step response to an SAA step (Table 4, p. 9): sigmoidal growth h = h_inf (1 - exp(-(t/tau)^n)); baseplate mean tau 17.6 h, t_infl 26.7 h, t90 66 h, t95 79 h, h_inf 13.9 mK per deg SAA; M1 tau 20.5 h, t_infl 38.2 h, t90 85 h; M2 tau 12.8 h, t_infl 45.9 h, t90 75 h (M2 first cools for about 15 h); rear-cavity optics (M3, FoM1-3, dichroic) t_infl about 22 h, tau about 20 h. Abstract quotes 21-46 h to enter the asymptotic regime. Folding mirrors begin responding 1-5 h before the baseplate.
- Temperatures: FoM3 128.6 K (coldest in cavity), M3 132.0 K, M2 109 K (heated), NISP detectors about 95 K, VIS CCDs 153 K (too warm for ice) (p. 10-12, 15).
- Ice-free ice thickness numbers: decontamination of 2024-03-14 removed about 78-156 nm (5.5-11 mg) of crystalline-like ice from FoM3; 2024-06-08 removed only 23-46 nm (1.6-3.2 mg) of amorphous, rough ice (p. 14-15). Total expected water reservoir about 100 g, mostly from 8 kg MLI (p. 10).
- VIS throughput: about 5% loss by Oct 2023 (pre-first decontamination), 12% by March 2024; after first decontamination (2024-03-14) fully restored (even about 5% above earliest data), then renewed rapid loss: 5% within 8 days, max 0.010 mag/day (blue, G_BP-G_RP = 0.75) after 34 days and 0.007 mag/day (red, 2.75) after 50 days, reaching 0.6 mag in source stars and strong spatial gradient: loss about 0.1 mag in upper right corner and > 0.5 mag in lower left of the FPA at 2024-06-08 (p. 12-13). Second (surgical) decontamination 2024-06-08 recovered 99% (red) and 98% (blue) relative to the first; since then stable to about 0.2 mmag/month; the red (blue) sources lost 0.5% (1.0%) in the 2.5 years since (Fig. 13 caption); end-to-end flux loss about 0.23 mmag/month (p. 26).
- Decontamination types: full thermal (PLM warmed to 200-270 K, about 2 weeks plus at least 2 weeks recalibration, possibly up to 2 months of PDC repeat; done once, July 2023 after launch) vs surgical (heat only FoM3, or FoM3 plus M3, from about 130 K to 160 K for 2 h, sublimes about 900 nm of ice; 3 days to stability, 8 days to full thermo-optical equilibration) (p. 10-11, Fig. A.1).
- NISP ice: growth about 9.9 nm/month predicted, measured 10.2 nm/month (about 378 nm accumulated on DET13 by Nov 2026); fringe amplitude e.g. 15.8% (LED A, 939 nm) and 9.4% (LED B, 1157 nm) peak-to-trough in flats on DET13; one full fringe needs 387 nm (A) / 488 nm (B) of ice (p. 15-16). No change in outgassing rate detected, so decontamination would only help briefly.
- Dichroic ghost in VIS: IE band reflected off the dichroic front surface; light transmitted to NISP reflects off the rear surface, producing a ghost offset by 70" from bright stars (G <~ 14) (p. 11). Ghost throughput has two peaks (503 nm and 961 nm) straddling the IE band; ghost colour m_blue - m_red = 1.84 (G_BP-G_RP) - 3.27; ghost strength rises steeply toward bluer colours, flattens to about 2.6e-4 for G_BP-G_RP >~ 1.8 (p. 12). 95% of G<14 stars have blue ghosts. In the ice age, ghosts were suppressed by up to factor about 5 more than the 0.6 mag loss in stars (strongly colour-dependent); nominal strength reappeared after 2024-06-08 (Fig. 14-15, p. 13).
- Zodiacal background (p. 16-19): self-calibration field ecliptic coordinates (lambda, beta) = (248.60, +88.6) deg, Galactic b = 30.3 deg, measured monthly on a single dither position (a field rotation of about 30 deg/month changes the scene); halo-orbit libration of the zodiacal signal detected at 8 sigma; models (ZodiPy/Kelsall for HE; ZodiSURF for IE, YE, JE) need scale factors s = 1.13-1.20 (Table 6); best-fit constant offset (ISL+OSL) b = 0.0013, 0.0027, 0.0058, 0.0115 MJy/sr in IE, YE, JE, HE, versus the conservative NDI prediction 0.0356, 0.0501, 0.0431, 0.0407, i.e. overestimates of factors 27, 19, 7, 4 (p. 18). ZodiPy coefficient switch within the HE band underestimates the HE model by 15-18% unless the band is split at 1.725 um.
- Background components in the self-cal field (Table 5, MJy/sr, IE/YE/JE/HE): EBL 0.00010/0.00032/0.00032/0.00036; diffuse Galactic light 0.0065/0.0064/0.0066/0.0070; mean instrumental background 0.0062/0.0037/0.0030/0.0031 (p. 18). Typical-survey DGL from Scaramella+2022: 0.0022 (IE), 0.0011 (JE) MJy/sr (p. 18). EBL from EUDF counts (3.4 deg^2; sources fainter than IE = 26.7 and 24.9 in YE/JE/HE; EUDF depth > 28 mag); an EBL 10x higher is an upper bound (p. 18).
- Guiding (p. 20-21, Table 8): 2611 h of 1 Hz data (3075 one-hour periods, 84% of on-sky time); RPE sigma = 8.4 mas (X), 4.72 mas (Y) tip/tilt axes and 193 mas around the boresight (Z); 99.7% containment [-42, 47] mas (X), [-28, 30] mas (Y), [-414, 471] mas (Z), well within 75/75/1500 mas allocations. VIS shutter motion (2.74 s open/close) kicks the pointing by up to 12 mas (about 0.1 VIS pixel), stabilising after 30-90 s (p. 21).
- Meteoroids (p. 23-27): 34 high-confidence impacts (Table 10) between Nov 2023 and Sept 2026; rate 1.5 per month after June 2025 (24 events); detected fraction 10-20% of all impacts; minimum detectable mass about 5e-7 g (78 um); flashes last about 1 ms (FWHM 1.2 and 0.8 ms); debris clouds decay with tau about 72 s. Strongest event 2026-07-08 shifted pointing by 1.3" (double sources) and deposited about 15 000 e-/pixel in NISP (equivalent to 5.2 AB mag/arcsec^2 over 1 ms) (p. 24-25). Expected rate for m >= 1e-9 g is 537/month over all faces (Table 11).

## Processing and methods
- Stray-light monitoring: stack 60-70 bias-corrected, gain-normalised darks per master dark, take median per quadrant, correct by (d_h/d_h0)^2, iterative linear regression for slopes (p. 4).
- Thermal modelling: grid in (AA, SAA), model y = M_c + A x(t) + C (t - t0) with x = (<d_h>/d_h)^2 - 1, solved by demeaning within cells; masks SAA >= 107 deg, 2 days after SAA steps >= 2 deg and dT/dt > 60 mK per 4 h; 3 sigma clipping (p. 7). Defocus measured from VIS diffraction spikes (Neumann et al. 2026) with precision 22 nm per measurement after decontamination (p. 7, 11).
- Ice detection: (i) per-image IE/G flux ratio of Gaia-matched stars in three G_BP-G_RP bins using static gains; sensitivity about 0.1 mmag/month trends; (ii) NISP monthly LED flats (5 LEDs, 939-1873 nm) fitted with AR-stack interference models; (iii) VIS ghost strength vs expectation; (iv) FGS guide-star flux during decontamination to infer ice thickness (sublimation flux integrated with Murphy and Koop vapour pressure) (p. 11-15).
- Zodiacal analysis: LE2 images, iterative outlier rejection, per-detector then per-exposure, converted with zero points; subtract EBL, DGL and time-variable instrumental background (VIS from master darks at matched attitude; NISP from 30-day rolling average of slew darks, i.e. persistence signal); fit offset b and scale s to models evaluated at the L2 halo position (p. 17-18).
- Guiding: stacks of RPE and torque telemetry; torques modelled with solar radiation pressure plus thermal-emission recoil (inverse-square distance dependence), p. 20-22, Appendix C.
- Impact search: screening of the on-board ramp-fit chi^2 and FGS/MPS signatures; no blind search yet (p. 23).

## Validation and performance
- Orbit/thermal: temperature modulation over an orbit only 31 mK (p. 29); required thermal stability met in nominal survey operations.
- Ice-removal efficacy: after both surgical decontaminations VIS transmission recovered to within 1-2% of the earlier state (p. 13); FoM3 reaches within 1.5 K of equilibrium 2.8 h after heating stops (Fig. A.1).
- Zodiacal model comparison: residuals have zero mean but are significantly non-zero because of field-rotation-dependent in-field stray light; halo-orbit libration signal detected at 8 sigma; models without rescaling reproduce 80-87% of the orbit-related amplitude, indicating OSL variable with time or inaccuracies in the Kelsall model (p. 19-20). Model ZB level about 0.09-0.15 MJy/sr in Fig. 20.
- Guiding statistics comfortably inside allocation (p. 21).

## Known issues, caveats, limitations
- Stray light (p. 4-6): attitude-dependent and increasing about 14%/yr; unknown additional entry paths remain; the pattern is the same open/closed shutter in the first order but not identical (p. 17). Highest in quadrant groups C and D and at higher SAA. Cosmological science is stated to be unaffected after nominal background subtraction, but LSB studies require dedicated background models (p. 28).
- VIS ice age 2024-03-14 to 2024-06-08: about 300 deg^2 of VIS data lost for DR1 (p. 28); heavy and spatially variable throughput loss and strong ghost suppression. A large FPA-dependent zero-point gradient developed after 30 days. The wavefront-error impact is still being analysed (p. 13). The surrounding thermal state was also unusual, so the defocus analysis starts after 2024-07-01 (p. 7-8).
- Continuing VIS drift: about 0.2 mmag/month (red 0.5% and blue 1.0% over 2.5 years); mix of contamination and area damage on the primary mirror, expected total about 9 mmag by mission end (p. 26).
- NISP QE modulation: interference fringes grow in time, bigger at shorter wavelengths and detector-dependent (DET13 strongest); affects YE and blue grism most (p. 15-16); correction under development for DR2/DR3.
- VIS dichroic ghost: 70" offset from the parent star, parent stars G <~ 14, blue stars give brighter ghosts; ghost strength changes with ice contamination, so masking by the nominal strength may be too generous for contaminated epochs (p. 11-13).
- Solar flare X-rays leave patterns on VIS detectors (affect about 2% of data; handled by the pipeline) (p. 6, 28). Radiation storms stop self-calibration visits and can force data loss (3 lost visits per the companion paper).
- Meteoroid impacts: exposures hit are lost; strongest impact produced image doubling (1.3" shift) and a flash of 15 000 e-/pixel. A shutter vibration anomaly limited FGS impact sensitivity before June 2025 (p. 25).
- Zodiacal background: absolute accuracy limited by the 4.5% absolute flux scale of ZodiSURF, dust-model completeness, and unquantified time-variable OSL; the old OSL map was computed in 2015 with a very conservative NDI and without the zodiacal contribution (p. 17-20). An updated Euclid zodiacal model is being developed (Maris et al. 2019 baseline).
- Defocus is not purely M2 displacement (diffraction-spike metric sensitive to higher-order WFE); attitude-driven defocus can step on mechanical relaxation events (jump on 2024-06-17 required a new VIS distortion model; p. 8, Fig. A.1).
- A typographical slip in p. 26 gives "2026-06-08" for the last decontamination; Fig. 13 and p. 13 give 2024-06-08. The paper also mentions 76 dither positions for the zodiacal measurement (p. 16) whereas the calibration paper describes 60 dithers (the zodiacal analysis uses one position).

## Implications for extended-galaxy photometry, 2-D modelling and low-surface-brightness work
- Background level and sky subtraction: the instrumental stray-light floor in VIS is about 3-12 x 10^-3 e-/s per quadrant group (Table 2) which compares with a zodiacal contribution about 7-10 times higher (stray light is 10-15% of ZB). Because it varies with AA/SAA, with detector position (groups A-D) and increases 14%/yr, the sky is spatially structured at the 10-15% (of zodiacal) level on detector scales and differs between epochs. For LSB work (outskirts at the 1% sky level or fainter) use local, tile-based or attitude-matched background models; the paper cites dedicated LSB background treatments (Cuillandre et al. 2025; Borlaff et al. 2022; Urbano et al. 2026; Kluge et al. 2025) and says the pipeline's nominal subtraction is enough only for cosmology (p. 28).
- Expected sky level: the zodiacal background is about 0.09-0.15 MJy/sr in the NEP self-cal field (Fig. 20; the instrumental and diffuse components are 0.003-0.012 and 0.0065 MJy/sr). Diffuse Galactic light (about 0.002 in IE for typical fields; up to 0.0065 MJy/sr at b = 30 deg) and EBL (0.0001-0.0004) are the astrophysical floors; cirrus fields near b = 30 deg need extra care for LSB.
- Out-of-field stray light is much lower than pre-launch NDI predictions (4-27x), so bright-star or Galactic scattered light is less of an issue than feared, although the true NDI wings are only constrained as upper limits; however, in-field stray light (scatter from bright stars in the FoV) was not quantified here beyond saying that the PSF is nearly diffraction-limited (Cuillandre et al. 2025).
- Ghosts: VIS dichroic ghost at 70" from stars brighter than G about 14 (the paper's "ghost strength" is a spectral-throughput integral ratio, 0.4-1.6 x 10^-4 in Fig. 15, not a directly measured ghost-to-star flux ratio; the calibration paper quotes an amplitude ratio requirement < 2e-6 for VIS). For nearby galaxies hosting bright stars or bright cores, check for compact ghosts about 70" from them, whose offset direction is fixed in the detector frame, and which are bluer-star strong. The paper notes ghosts are compact and few (p. 17). In contaminated-epoch data the ghost is suppressed, so masks tuned to the nominal strength are conservative.
- Photometric stability: VIS throughput is stable to about 0.2 mmag/month since June 2024, but data taken in Oct 2023-Jun 2024 (heavy contamination, large FPA-dependent zero-point gradient) were excluded from DR1; if a target appears in early data, check that the area belongs to the retained set. NISP photometry has time-variable QE (YE worst), so multi-epoch/colour comparisons must account for the calibration epoch.
- PSF/focus: defocus varies with SAA (z about 0.32-0.56 um) and settles over 1-3 days after attitude changes (t90 of 3-3.5 days for baseplate), so the PSF of a tile observed after a large SAA step (e.g. coming from the EDF-S to EWS) may be less representative; the VIS PSF FWHM is stated in the companion paper as 0.14". The thermal drift is small during nominal survey operations. Jitter: RPE sigma of 5-8 mas (tip/tilt) is negligible (<0.1 VIS pixel), so guiding does not broaden the PSF; the shutter kick (12 mas for tens of seconds) is similarly negligible.
- Transient artefacts: a meteoroid hit can double sources by 1.3" or add flash/debris light across a whole NISP exposure (to 15 000 e-/pixel); such exposures are flagged invalid, but unflagged smaller events could leave low-level diffuse offsets in single NISP exposures (the paper only notes that VIS is less sensitive).
- Solar X-ray patterns on VIS are a possible source of large-scale (sunshield-shaped) artefacts in affected exposures; the pipeline rejects affected areas.

## Practical recommendations for users
- Do not rely on the global sky solution for LSB: model the background per tile or per exposure, ideally with knowledge of the AA/SAA of each observation (metadata) because stray light changes with attitude and with epoch (+14%/yr).
- Prefer data taken after 2024-06-08 (retained in DR1) and be aware that epoch affects VIS throughput at the 1% level and NISP QE at up to 0.1 mag (YE).
- Mask or inspect regions about 70" from stars with G < 14 for dichroic ghosts; remember the ghost position is tied to the optics (fixed offset), the strength is colour dependent and was suppressed during Mar-Jun 2024.
- When modelling extended galaxies near the edges of a field or near bright stars, expect residual stray light of order 10% of the zodiacal level; use a high-order sky model or masks of bright-star halos.
- Use zodiacal-light expectations (Kelsall/ZodiSURF with the scale factors s about 1.13-1.20) only as priors, not for absolute subtraction.
- For multi-band (VIS-NISP) colour work, account for the different time dependence of the two instruments (stable VIS; NISP QE drifting).
